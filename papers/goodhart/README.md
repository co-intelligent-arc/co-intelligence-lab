# Goodhart論文

**Full title:** When Proxies Become Purposes: Reconnection Failure and Goodhartization in Proxy-Based Institutions

**Authors:** Yuki Tanaka  
**Date:** 2026  
**Status:** Final draft complete. Venue targeting in progress.

## What this is

A conceptual framework for understanding how proxy-based institutions lose the capacity to revise their own proxies. The paper reframes Goodhart's law not as a simple corruption-under-pressure story, but as a failure of the reconnection circuit through which proxy inadequacy is noticed, articulated, institutionally taken up, and made revisable.

**Key concepts:**

- **Goodhartization:** the process by which proxies introduced to make complex purposes tractable become stabilized as ends in their own right — distinct from Goodhart's law proper, which focuses on what goes wrong with the proxy, not what prevents correction
- **Reconnection failure:** the weakening of the circuit through which proxy inadequacy is recognized, articulated, taken up institutionally, and made revisable
- **Reconnection-cost avoidance:** the mechanism by which failure becomes durable; revision is costly, unevenly distributed, and locally rational to defer — so institutions continue to rely on a proxy not because it remains adequate but because correcting it is expensive

**AI implication:** AI systems that optimize existing proxies more efficiently may intensify this dynamic. But deliberate design may also reduce some costs of reconnection — opening the possibility of AI as a tool of reconnection rather than optimization alone.

**Connection to the broader theory:** Goodhartization is a concrete instantiation of Region B at the institutional scale. The reconnection circuit is the institution-level equivalent of the recomputation circuit; reconnection failure is the institutional form of M's collapse.

## Core claim

> The problem is not that institutions use proxies. The problem is that they can lose the capacity to revise them. Goodhartization occurs not when a proxy is imperfect — all proxies are imperfect — but when the circuit that reconnects a proxy to the purpose by which it was introduced or justified stops functioning.
