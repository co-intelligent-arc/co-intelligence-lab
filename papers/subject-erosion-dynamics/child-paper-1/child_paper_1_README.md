## Overview

This paper establishes the conceptual foundations for the series. It proposes a functional redefinition of subjectivity and formalizes a three-tier distinction among subject, quasi-subject, and non-subject — not as ontological kinds, but as context-dependent functional states.

The **operational core definition** proposed here:

> A subject is an entity that, while sustaining constraints across multiple hierarchical levels, is capable of updating the coherence between itself and its relational context through recomputation.

Two central assumptions of modern subject theory are suspended — individual-completeness and static unity — while recomputability, attributability of responsibility, and continuity of identity are retained.

A key concept introduced here is **preservative upward integration**: the operative mode by which a subject incorporates higher-order constraints into recomputation without dissolving its individual-level constraints. This is a structural description, not a normative imperative.

The paper also draws the critical distinction between **Region B** (a state in which structural stability and cognitive autonomy remain while recomputation has ceased) and **subject-injury** (the process leading toward that state). Misclassifying Region B as non-subjecthood forecloses intervention; recognizing it as an arrested process preserves the possibility of recovery.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Subject** | An entity capable of updating the coherence between itself and its relational context through recomputation while sustaining constraints across multiple hierarchical levels |
| **Quasi-subject** | An entity in a state in which one or more of D, M, and T_eval have ceased or declined in function while the possibility of recovery is preserved because at least one condition remains |
| **Non-subject** | An entity in a state in which all of D, M, and T_eval have ceased to function, and no residual condition remains as a foothold for recovery |
| **Subject-injury** | The process of transition toward Region B — not non-subjecthood, but the loss of recomputability |
| **Preservative upward integration** | The recomputation mode that introduces higher-order constraints without dissolving individual-level constraints |
| **Functional redefinition** | The overall approach of this paper: defining subjectivity through functional conditions rather than ontological kinds |
| **Operational core definition** | The central definitional claim of the paper, from which the three-tier distinction is derived |
