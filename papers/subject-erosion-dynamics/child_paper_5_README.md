## Overview

This paper describes subject-injury not as a malfunction internal to individuals but as a dynamic reproduced by institutional design. Building on the minimal causal chain formalized in Child Paper 2, it introduces a **second pathway** into subject-injury: the designed simplification of evaluative space, which operates independently of rising conformity pressure and can deplete D (difference-retention) through the reduction of the evaluative axis to a single dimension alone.

The paper develops this claim across three domains:

- **Education**: single-axis evaluation systems structurally mass-produce Region B, with high achievers positioned closest to Region B — maintaining T_cog output while T_eval and T_val are eroded
- **Clinical**: T_val fixation — in which value frameworks cease to update while the subjective sense of autonomy is preserved — can form a mutually amplifying loop with depressive promotion, mediated by institutional design
- **Institutional**: subject protection is formalized as a dynamic necessary condition (not an ethical add-on), expressed as four rights and four principles in a multiplicative structure

The central conclusion: subject-injury persists not by breaking down but by appearing to function well. Subject protection is therefore the institutional condition for detecting — and interrupting — the cessation that wears the face of success.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Second pathway** | The designed simplification of evaluative space: subject-injury arising independently of rising conformity pressure C, through the reduction of evaluative axes to a single dimension |
| **Social reproduction** | The process by which the same evaluative structure is repeated across generations and institutional cycles, self-reinforced by the institution's own success indicators — "reproduction without intent" |
| **Single-axis evaluation** | Any educational institution operating a measurable single evaluative axis as its primary selection criterion (standardized tests, GPA-only progression, etc.) |
| **T_val fixation** | The state in which value frameworks cease to update while the subjective sense of autonomy is preserved — consistent with Region B, distinct from loss of subjective autonomy |
| **Depressive promotion** | The structural condition in which T_val fixation produces a sense of action-ineffectiveness, which in turn reinforces T_val fixation in a mutually amplifying loop |
| **Subject protection** | The institutional necessary condition for sustaining a recomputable society — not an ethical add-on but a dynamic requirement |
| **Four rights** | Right to delay · Right to dissent · Right to withdraw · Right to non-optimization (institutional language, subject-side) |
| **Four principles** | Two-tier evaluation design · Institutional legitimation of suspension · Recording and preservation of difference · Tolerance of partial non-participation (institutional design language) |
| **Multiplicative structure** | The structure in which the absence of any one of the four rights/principles renders the others functionally inoperative |

---

## The Four Rights and Four Principles

The four rights translate the dynamic conditions into subject-side demands; the four principles translate them into institutional design requirements. They are the same content at two levels of abstraction.

| Right | Principle | Dynamic condition addressed |
|-------|-----------|---------------------------|
| Right to delay | Institutional legitimation of suspension | Securing H (suspension capacity) |
| Right to dissent | Recording and preservation of difference | Maintaining D (difference-retention) |
| Right to withdraw | Tolerance of partial non-participation | Counter to single-axis over-optimization |
| Right to non-optimization | Two-tier evaluation design | Protecting T_val updateability |

---

## Testable Implication

A testable implication of this framework is that institutions dominated by single-axis evaluation will show stable high-output performance together with declining records of suspension, dissent, and alternative framing. A minimum observational protocol would track whether delay, dissent, withdrawal, and non-optimization remain institutionally recordable without penalty across time.
