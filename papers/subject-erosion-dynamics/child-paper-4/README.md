## Overview

This paper proposes a descriptive framework — not a strict application of physical law — for understanding why difference-preserving arrangements tend to be displaced by difference-reducing ones under specific institutional cost structures.

Using thermodynamic analogy as **descriptive scaffolding**, it reorganizes the variables developed in this series (D, Var(φ), E_r_eff) into a schematic map of closure tendencies and points of intervention across three scales: individual, institutional, and civilizational.

The central distinction is between **closure** and **homogenization**:
- **Closure** = the deterioration of input conditions (supply conditions for D become impoverished)
- **Homogenization** = the downstream consequence (Var(φ) contracts)

Closure is therefore a matter of inlet conditions; homogenization is the state change that follows. This distinction makes the points of intervention visible: intervening upstream in closure is different from — and often more effective than — intervening downstream in homogenization.

The paper concludes that intervention must target not only the supply of difference, but also the **institutional cost structures that make closure the default trajectory**.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Closure** | The deterioration of input conditions — the decreasing availability of difference inputs capable of disrupting existing frames; the upstream condition |
| **Homogenization** | The downstream consequence of closure: the contraction of evaluative diversity Var(φ) |
| **Closure tendency** | The structural tendency for difference-eliminating arrangements to be selected as the lower-cost option under prevailing institutional designs |
| **Descriptive scaffolding** | The use of thermodynamic analogy not as strict physical law but as auxiliary vocabulary for clarifying structural correspondences |
| **Civilizational Region B** | An analytical construct describing the state in which aggregate output and functional stability are maintained while the capacity for frame-level renewal is collectively diminished — not an ontological claim |
| **Design responsibility** | The accumulated responsibility for the cost structures that make closure the default trajectory; distributed across individual, institutional, and civilizational design choices |
| **Points of intervention** | The identifiable locations in the schematic map where design change can alter the cost structure maintaining closure |
