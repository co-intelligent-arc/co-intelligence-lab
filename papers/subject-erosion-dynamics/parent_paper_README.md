## Overview

This paper provides the conceptual foundation and overall map of questions for a series of papers on subject theory in the AI age. It is not a summary of the companion papers but a schematic map of questions — one that connects subject definition, relational dynamics, institutional reproduction, and civilizational scope into a single theoretical trajectory.

The central claim: **subject-injury does not appear as destruction. It proceeds as a process in which only internal updating ceases while surface functioning is preserved.** The prototypical state of this process is **Region B** — apparently normal, internally halted — in which T_cog remains while M, T_eval, and T_val have ceased.

Subject protection is formalized not as an ethical add-on but as the **institutional necessary condition** for interrupting the reproduction cycle — specifically, the cycle in which institutions misread Region B as achievement, reinforce the same design, and supply the same conditions to the next cohort.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Subject** | An entity capable of updating coherence through recomputation while taking on the constraints of multiple hierarchical levels |
| **Region B** | State: σ and T_cog remain while M, T_eval, and T_val have ceased — apparently normal, internally halted |
| **Subject-injury** | Process: the transition into Region B — not destruction but invisible degradation |
| **Recomputability** | The capacity to call existing judgments, evaluations, and actions into question and update them |
| **Designed simplification of evaluative space** | Second pathway: the static narrowing of evaluative criteria that depletes D independently of conformity pressure |
| **Social reproduction** | The cycle in which institutions misread Region B as achievement, reinforce design, and re-supply the same conditions |
| **Subject protection** | Institutional necessary condition for interrupting the reproduction cycle — not an ethical add-on |
| **Four principles** | Two-tier evaluation design · Institutional legitimation of suspension · Recording and preservation of difference · Tolerance of partial non-participation |
| **Multiplicative structure** | The structure in which the absence of any one of the four principles renders the others functionally inoperative |
| **Preservative upward integration (抑我)** | Mode of recomputation that preserves individual constraints while introducing upper-level constraints — structural description, not normative command |

## Three Established Claims

**Claim A (Concept):** The subject is defined not as individual-complete but as an entity that, in dependence on the relational field while taking on the constraints of multiple hierarchical levels, can update coherence through recomputation.

**Claim B (Dynamics):** Subject-injury is not destruction but invisible degradation — the disappearance of difference, the cessation of recomputation, and the paralysis of evaluative autonomy proceeding in overlapping fashion — whose prototypical state is Region B.

**Claim C (Design):** Interrupting the social reproduction of subject-injury requires as a necessary condition an institutional design in which the four principles are simultaneously satisfied as a multiplicative structure.
