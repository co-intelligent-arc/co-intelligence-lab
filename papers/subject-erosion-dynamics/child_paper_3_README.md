## Overview

Relational knowledge functions both as a condition for subject formation and as a medium through which subject-injury propagates. This paper formalizes a **third pathway** into subject-injury, distinct from rising conformity pressure (Child Paper 2) and the institutional simplification of evaluative space (Child Paper 5): the **asymmetry of subject intensity** within a relational field.

The central concept is **well-intentioned erosion**: subject-injury driven not by coercion but by the relational outsourcing of recomputation through trust and cost minimization. When ΔS_r (subject intensity difference) is large, the lower-intensity party is drawn toward a cost-minimizing path in which adopting the higher-intensity party's frame reduces the burden of maintaining its own difference-retention — even in relationships of good intent, care, and support.

Trust is therefore neither inherently generative nor inherently erosive. It functions as an **accelerant in either direction** depending on whether bidirectionality of difference supply is structurally maintained.

## Key Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Relational knowledge** | The process through which the interaction of multiple subjects gives rise to a form of recomputability that no individual could have generated alone |
| **Subject intensity (S_r)** | A relationally dependent composite index summarizing the degree to which a subject maintains D, M, and T_eval in a relatively independent way within a given relational field |
| **Subject intensity difference (ΔS_r)** | The operative variable: not which subject is stronger, but which is structurally dependent on the other's frame |
| **Well-intentioned erosion** | Subject-injury driven not through coercion but through the interaction of trust and cost minimization, resulting in the continuous relational outsourcing of recomputation |
| **Third pathway** | The entry condition independent of rising conformity pressure and institutional simplification; operates even when C is low and simplification is absent |
| **Bidirectionality** | The structural condition distinguishing generative support from well-intentioned erosion |
| **Scaffolding** | Temporary use of another's frame while maintaining one's own D — part of a generative direction, not erosion |

### Three co-occurring signs of well-intentioned erosion

1. Sustained decline in self-initiated judgment
2. Unidirectional difference supply
3. Asymmetric suspension of evaluation

Single instances of dependency constitute scaffolding; the **repeated co-occurrence** of all three signs constitutes erosion.
