# Generative AI, Cognitive Offloading, and Question Formation

**Full title:** Generative AI, Cognitive Offloading, and Question Formation: A Conceptual Model of Question-Regeneration Displacement in Human-AI Interaction

**Authors:** Yuki Tanaka  
**Date:** 2026  
**Status:** Draft complete. Submitted to *AI & Society* (Springer Nature, Open Forum). Cover letter dated June 8, 2026.

---

## What this is

A conceptual paper that identifies a specific and undertheorized risk of generative AI use: not the erosion of the ability to produce correct answers, but the displacement of the process by which humans regenerate questions as their own.

**Key constructs:**

- **Question regeneration:** the process by which a user notices a discrepancy, holds it as an unresolved problem, reformulates it, and takes ownership of it as their own question
- **Question-regeneration displacement:** when this process — rather than remaining user-held — is progressively assumed by the AI-mediated interactional system; AI supplies polished framings and smoothed questions before the user has undergone the friction of regenerating the question themselves
- **Four-layer operational model of AI-mediated thinking:** question formation / hypothesis construction / processing / recalculation — decomposed to identify where displacement occurs
- **Scaffold vs. substitute distinction:** AI-generated questions that function as scaffolds for the user's regenerative process are distinct from those that function as substitutes for it — the paper specifies scope conditions for when substitution is more likely

The paper draws on three literatures: epistemic agency and question-asking, cognitive offloading and critical thinking, and evaluation shift and overreliance. It identifies a cyclical dimension missing from all three: whether the notice-hold-reformulate-own loop remains user-held under AI mediation.

**Connection to the broader theory:** question-regeneration displacement is an instance of Region B at the cognitive level — T_cog is preserved (the user continues to interact fluently) while T_eval and T_val are eroded (the user no longer regenerates their own evaluative standpoint). The paper provides an independent empirical and conceptual pathway into the same structural diagnosis.

## Core claim

> The risk of generative AI is not merely that users come to rely on AI-generated answers. It is that the system may displace the very process by which humans regenerate their own questions — and this displacement is not captured by existing accounts of overreliance, epistemic agency, or cognitive offloading.
