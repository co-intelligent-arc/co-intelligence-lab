# Recomputation Dynamics — Summary

**Title:** Recomputation Dynamics: A General Theory of Adaptive Capacity
**Current version:** v0.3.4
**Role in the trilogy:** General theory core
**Target journal:** Entropy (primary), Complex Systems (backup)

## Overview

This paper proposes a general theory of adaptive capacity based on two jointly necessary functions: exploration and updating. Adaptive capacity is formalized as a multiplicative structure, where decline emerges when either exploratory breadth or update depth collapses.

## Core Claim

Adaptive capacity is not a scalar resource but a recomputational process. Systems remain viable only when exploration and updating remain jointly functional. The failure of either is sufficient to trigger metastability and eventual collapse.

## Working Formulation

```
A(e, u) = f(e) × g(u)
```

The multiplicative structure encodes a bottleneck logic: high u cannot compensate for collapsed e, and vice versa. This distinguishes RD from additive models that allow compensatory trade-offs.

## Main Contribution

The paper provides a scale-general framework connecting individual, organizational, and social adaptation. It introduces the conceptual basis for:

- Metastability regions (A / B / C)
- Update hierarchy
- Hysteresis and recovery asymmetry
- Update inertia debt

## Relation to OMD

OMD functions as an illustrative empirical case of the broader RD theory. In that mapping, industrial diversity (Shannon entropy) corresponds to e, and institutional update capacity (residual Δe) corresponds to u.

## Relation to RLAF

At the individual scale, RLAF's autonomy tensor T provides the micro-foundation for u. Recomputation capacity at the organizational level (M) aggregates over individual-level u values.

## Current Status

The theoretical skeleton is strong and the paper reads as a coherent general-theory manuscript. However, some of the most important claims still need stronger formal defense.
