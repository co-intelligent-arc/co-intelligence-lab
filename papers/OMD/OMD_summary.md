# Organizational Metastability Dynamics — Summary

**Title:** Recomputation Capacity and Hysteresis in Social Adaptation: Diagnostic Limits of Linear Metrics
**Current version:** v0.9
**Role in the trilogy:** Empirical anchor
**Target journal:** Chaos (submitted: CHA26-AR-00576)

## Overview

This paper develops a model of organizational adaptive capacity using two core dimensions: exploratory diversity (e) and update capacity (u). It examines 1,908 Japanese municipalities and tests whether adaptive capacity predicts demographic outcomes.

## Core Claim

Net in-migration is not a reliable proxy for adaptive capacity. Municipalities can exhibit demographic recovery while their internal adaptive engine has already stalled.

## Key Concept

**Demographic buffering effect:** the tendency for external population inflow to temporarily mask latent declines in internal adaptive capacity. This effect is path-dependent and consistent with OMD's hysteresis prediction — once a municipality becomes reliant on residential inflow as a signal of stability, the incentive to invest in recomputation diminishes.

## Formalization

- e = industrial diversity (Shannon entropy, normalized)
- u = update capacity (residual of Δe orthogonalized against e)
- A(e, u) = e_norm × u_norm
- Regions A / B / C defined by median split on (e_norm, u_norm)

## Empirical Result

Contrary to simple prediction, low-e/low-u municipalities (Region C) exhibited the highest net in-migration (r = −0.458, p < 0.001). Sensitivity analysis excluding top 3%, 5%, and 10% of municipalities by employment size yielded virtually identical results (r = −0.455 to −0.462), ruling out urban agglomeration as the explanation.

## Main Contribution

The paper shows that apparent demographic vitality may coexist with structural adaptive decline. This result strengthens the theoretical claim that deterioration often becomes visible only after the internal update system has already weakened — a concrete empirical illustration of Region B invisibility.
