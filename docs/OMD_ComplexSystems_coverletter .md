# Cover Letter — Complex Systems

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration the manuscript "Recomputation Capacity and Hysteresis in Social Adaptation: Diagnostic Limits of Linear Metrics," which we believe is well suited to *Complex Systems*.

The paper addresses a foundational problem in the dynamics of social adaptation: the failure of conventional linear indicators to detect the silent crossing of hysteretic phase boundaries. Using a dynamical framework in which adaptive capacity is governed by the interaction of structural commitment, recomputation capacity, and value-direction variance, we derive a phase structure with three dynamical regions and characterize the conditions under which systems enter a locked-in regime from which recovery is asymmetric and path-dependent. The model's qualitative behavior is consistent with dynamics expected near a saddle-node-type transition, and the hysteresis prediction follows directly from the resulting conceptual phase-space geometry.

We evaluate the framework empirically using municipal governance data from 1,908 Japanese municipalities. The analysis reveals a structural negative association between adaptive capacity and net in-migration (r = −0.458), which persists robustly across sensitivity analyses excluding large employment centers (r = −0.455 to −0.462). We interpret this reversal as an instance of what we term the *demographic buffering effect* — a masking mechanism through which external population inflow suppresses observable indicators of internal adaptive decline, consistent with the model's prediction of Region B invisibility.

This result speaks directly to the diagnostic limits of linear metrics in complex systems: a social system can appear demographically functional precisely because, and while, its internal recomputation capacity has already stalled.

We believe this work contributes to *Complex Systems* in the following respects. First, it extends nonlinear dynamical modeling to the institutional adaptation domain, characterizing a conceptual phase-space geometry and hysteresis structure from a minimal three-variable system. Second, it provides an empirical test of model predictions at national scale. Third, it introduces a formally defined masking mechanism — demographic buffering — that connects observable demographic dynamics to latent adaptive state in a way that standard linear indicators cannot.

The manuscript has not been previously published and is not under consideration elsewhere. We have no conflicts of interest to declare.

We thank the editors for their consideration.

Sincerely,
Yuki Tanaka
Independent Researcher
Seto Inland Sea, Japan
