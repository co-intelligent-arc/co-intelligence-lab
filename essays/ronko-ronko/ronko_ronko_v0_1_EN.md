# Essay on Essays
## — What Does It Mean to Think? Agency, Subordination, and Relational Knowledge in the Age of AI —

Yuki Tanaka
March 2026

---

## Preface: Why Reflect on Reflection Now?

Most people treat "thinking" as a single act.

In reality, thinking decomposes into at least four distinct operations: formulating a question, provisionally constructing hypotheses, processing coherently within given constraints, and returning to re-examine the question itself in light of what the processing revealed.

These four operations are fundamentally different from one another. And AI acts on each of them in fundamentally different ways.

The problem in the age of AI is not that machines process faster than humans. The problem is that humans can no longer see how far they have outsourced the core of thinking, precisely because they have stopped distinguishing these four layers.

Concern that "thinking capacity is declining" has been raised before — every time a new technology appeared. In that sense, the concern about AI is not new. But this time, something is structurally different, not merely quantitatively larger.

To grasp that difference, we must decompose "thinking."

This essay re-examines the act of deliberation itself, providing a foundational map that brings both the erosion of agency and the emergence of relational knowledge onto the same terrain.

---

## Chapter 1: Thinking Is Not One Thing

**Central proposition: Thinking is not one thing. It must be decomposed into four layers — question-formation, hypothesis-construction, computational processing, and recalculation — and recalculation in particular must be understood as a circuit that regenerates new questions from the configurations produced by processing.**

### 1.1 The Four Layers Defined

When we decompose thinking, at least four layers become visible.

**Note: These four layers are not sequential real-world stages of cognition but an operational decomposition for analytical purposes.** In the actual brain, these processes run in parallel and interpenetrate. The distinctions drawn here are operational concepts for analyzing where and how AI acts on thinking — they do not claim to exist as independent modules in the brain.

**L1: Question-Formation**

Deciding what to ask. This is the operation of setting the frame of the problem itself.

Everyday example: At work, asking "Why is this project delayed?" versus "Why does this team always run late?" sends subsequent thinking in entirely different directions. The question itself determines the scope of all processing that follows.

**L2: Hypothesis-Construction**

Provisionally placing possible structures and possibilities. The operation of generating candidate answers.

Everyday example: "The delay might be due to resource shortage. Or perhaps the scope is ambiguous. Or perhaps decision-making is slow." At this stage, nothing is yet resolved to a single candidate.

**L3: Computational Processing**

Coherently processing toward a conclusion under given constraints.

Everyday example: "Assuming resource shortage, can the project be completed with current headcount and hours?" "Assuming scope ambiguity, where exactly in the requirements definition is the problem?" Taking a hypothesis as a premise and reasoning through it logically.

**L4: Recalculation**

Detecting gaps in the configuration produced by L3, and regenerating a new L1 from those gaps. Thinking is updated primarily at this layer.

Everyday example: "I assumed resource shortage and ran the calculation, but actually headcount is sufficient. That means the question might have been wrong. The real question is: 'Why are we late even though we have enough people?'" — The question itself is updated.

Summarizing the four layers:

| Layer | Name | Content | Measured by institutions? |
|-------|------|---------|--------------------------|
| L1 | Question-Formation | Deciding what to ask | Hard to measure |
| L2 | Hypothesis-Construction | Provisionally placing possibilities | Hard to measure |
| L3 | Computational Processing | Deriving conclusions from conditions | Easy to measure |
| L4 | Recalculation | Detecting gaps in configurations; regenerating L1 | Hard to measure |

### 1.2 What Educational and Social Institutions Have Evaluated

School tests, performance reviews, professional examinations — most of these have measured L3. Questions are given in advance; candidate hypotheses are presented as multiple-choice options; recalculation is processed as "correction." L1, L2, and L4 are rarely evaluated.

This is not an accident. L3 is easy to measure, compare, and grade. L1, L2, and L4 are difficult to measure, with no single correct answer. So institutions converged on L3.

The important point is that this was not malicious. Institutions converged on L3 for rational reasons. But as a consequence, opportunities to exercise L1, L2, and L4 have been systematically eroded.

AI's takeover of L3 has exposed this bias for the first time.

### 1.3 The Essence of L4: Gap Detection and L1 Regeneration

Among the four layers, our understanding of L4 needs the most revision.

The old understanding treated L4 as "the stage of revisiting L3's answers and moving to alternative possibilities." But this captures only the surface of L4.

L4's essence is deeper:

```
L3 arranges something (produces a configuration)
↓
That configuration makes gaps, absences, and distortions visible
↓
L4 detects those gaps
↓
The gaps become new L1 questions
```

L4 is not merely "doubting the answer" but **a circuit that regenerates questions.**

When baking a donut, you do not make the hole directly. The hole emerges as a result of configuring the dough. L4 has the same structure. When the "dough" of L3's output is arranged, a "hole" that was previously invisible emerges. That hole becomes a new L1.

From the workplace example: "I ran the calculation assuming resource shortage, and the numbers all check out — but I still cannot explain why things are delayed." This incoherence appears in L3's output. This incoherence is the gap. L4 generates from it a new L1: "Perhaps the question was wrong."

One important supplement: gaps are potentially infinite — every incoherence, every sense of unease is a candidate gap. L4 detects gaps, but **which gap to adopt as a question** lies outside the L1–L4 operations themselves. It depends on the subject's value field: sense of responsibility, bodily unease, on-the-ground sensibility — what counts as worth asking. This field problem is placed in Chapter 7 as the L0 (field of questioning) auxiliary hypothesis.

### 1.4 Which Layers AI Can and Cannot Substitute

Given this understanding of the four layers, we can now see AI's action with precision.

Current AI is overwhelmingly strong at L3. Deriving coherent conclusions from given premises is what AI does best.

L2 can be partially assisted. AI can draw from training data the hypotheses most commonly constructed for a given question. But the nature of this assistance requires scrutiny in later chapters.

L1 appears assistable. But here the first trap lies.

L4 — particularly the L4→L1 regeneration circuit — risks being short-circuited by dependence on AI. This chapter's central problem is addressed in Chapter 4.

One point to flag now: AI offering question candidates and humans regenerating questions through L4 appear superficially similar but are structurally entirely different operations.

## Chapter 2: What Is Deliberation?

**Central proposition: Deliberation is not the processing of a given question but a multi-layered activity capable of detecting gaps in the configuration of processed results and regenerating the questions themselves.**

### 2.1 The Difference Between Deliberation and Processing

Processing is the completion of L3. A question is given, a procedure exists, and progress moves toward a correct answer. The completion of processing is "producing the optimal answer to this question."

Deliberation is different. Deliberation begins at L1, passes through L2 to execute L3, then returns to L1 via L4. It moves back and forth. And L1 can change. Recognizing during deliberation that "the question itself was wrong" can be the most essential product of deliberation.

The completion of processing, then, is "producing an answer." The completion of deliberation may be "departing with a better question."

This sounds strange at first. Deliberation without an answer? But looking at the history of science, the most important deliberations have derived their value from updating the question. From "Why does the apple fall?" to "Why doesn't the moon fall?" From "Why do people get sick?" to "Why don't people get sick?" — updates to the question have been the advance of thought.

### 2.2 When Deliberation Ceases to Be Deliberation

Deliberation can exist in form while being processing in substance. The symptoms are:

- The question is fixed from the outset and never questioned
- Only one hypothesis begins the inquiry; no alternative hypotheses are generated
- When processing ends, deliberation ends (L4 does not activate)
- "Why was this question framed this way?" is never asked

In this state, the form of deliberation exists, but the circuit L1→L2→L3→L4→L1' is not turning. The repetition of L3 is called "deliberation."

Among academic papers, corporate strategy documents, and policy proposals, there is no shortage of material that is "closer to processing than deliberation" in this sense. The question is given at the outset and carried through to the end unchanged.

### 2.3 What Deliberation Requires

For deliberation to turn the L4→L1 circuit, three functional conditions are necessary:

- **H (Holding capacity)**: Not closing prematurely when L3 produces an answer. Not ending with "the answer is out."
- **D (Difference retention)**: Not eliminating other possibilities generated at L2. Even after adopting one hypothesis, not forgetting the others.
- **R (Reorientation)**: Returning to L1 via L4 and reconfiguring the question. Continuously retaining the possibility that "the question may have been off."

Crucially, these three are also the functions most easily eroded by dependence on AI. H is eroded by adaptation to speed; D by the inclination toward coherence; R by the reinforcement of confirmation bias.

## Chapter 3: What Is Agency?

**Central proposition: Agency is not the possession of will but the functional state of retaining differences and being capable of recalculating beyond the current answer.**

### 3.1 Working Definition of Agency

"Agency" carries different meanings in philosophy, psychology, education, and law. This essay provides a working definition appropriate to the context of Essay on Essays, while acknowledging those broader discussions.

Working definition of agency in this essay:

> Agency is the state of being functionally capable of retaining differences and recalculating beyond the current answer.

What matters is that this is not a question of "possessing will" or "deciding freely." Those may be conditions for agency, but the operational question here is simpler: "Is L4 functioning?"

Using this working definition, the degree of agency can be approximated as:

**Degree of Agency ≈ Recalculation Capacity × Recalculation Resources**

Recalculation capacity: Being in the functional state to execute L4  
Recalculation resources: The supply of difference (D), holding (H), and reorientation (R) necessary for L4

If either is extremely low, degree of agency falls. Capacity without resources means recalculation does not start. Resources without capacity means differences are encountered but passed over.

One further note on H (Holding capacity): H is not simply "not rushing to an answer." In dialogue with AI, H is called to perform a more specific resistance — the moment L4 detects a gap, AI offers a known filling: "Perhaps it is this?" At that moment, H must function as the force that refuses the friendly filling and holds the gap as a gap.

This is called **gap-holding capacity (Negative Capability)**: the endurance to remain in the darkness of the undetermined, refusing the AI's statistically "most plausible answer" as an early closure. Gap-holding capacity is central to H for L4→L1 regeneration to occur.

One response to the objection that this working definition is "too functional": This essay does not argue for agency as a metaphysical entity (soul or free will) but defines it as the operational structure supporting recalculation capacity. This definition does not directly guarantee human uniqueness. But that is not the purpose. The question this essay asks is "what structure must be maintained to count as agentic?" not "can only humans possess agency?" The question of human uniqueness is treated as a separate inquiry into what value field, embodiment, and formative history maintain this functional structure.

### 3.2 The Relationship Between Agency Erosion and L4

Agency erosion is not a general deterioration of thinking. Its core is the process by which L3 remains while L4 stops. This corresponds to the phenomenon described elsewhere as "Region B" — output continues but recalculation does not occur.

An important distinction: agency erosion is often first observed as L4 stopping while L3 remains intact. In most cases, the erosion of L1 and L2 follows from this.

Why does L4 tend to stop first?

L3 is easily evaluated externally, and external evaluation drives its development. L4 is difficult to evaluate externally, and functions resistant to external evaluation tend to atrophy. Moreover, when one stops running L3 for oneself, the very experience of detecting gaps in configurations diminishes. This double pressure tends to stop L4 first.

### 3.3 Possessing Will Is Not Sufficient for Agency

The sense of "deciding for myself" and whether L4 is functioning are independent questions.

A state in which L4 is not activating while one feels one is deciding for oneself is entirely possible. The value frame is fixed and only selections repeat. This can be called "the inner sensation of agency erosion."

Take someone who, each morning, chooses from "recommended content" on their smartphone while feeling they are choosing. But if the design of the options holds L1, the act of choosing is merely the execution of L3. For L4 to activate, the question "Why do I keep choosing this kind of content?" must arise.

This is happening a great deal in contemporary daily life.

## Chapter 4: Where Does AI Dependence and Subordination Invade Thinking?

**Central proposition: AI does not invade thinking uniformly across all layers. It acts differently on each of the four layers. The most dangerous risk is that AI's statistical question proposals replace the circuit by which L4 regenerates new L1 questions from configurations.**

### 4.1 Distinguishing Dependence from Subordination

"AI dependence" and "AI subordination" must be distinguished:

| Concept | Definition | Nature |
|---------|------------|--------|
| AI dependence | Borrowing processing (L3) | A matter of degree. Can serve as scaffolding if used well. |
| AI subordination | Questions (L1), hypotheses (L2), and recalculation (L4) being held by the external | A matter of phase. Agency erosion begins here. |

**Dependence is a matter of degree; subordination is a matter of phase.**

Using a calculator to calculate and asking a calculator "what should I calculate?" are entirely different operations. The former is L3 dependence; the latter is movement toward L1 subordination. The same act of "using AI" can have opposite effects on thinking.

### 4.2 How AI Acts on Each Layer

| Layer | When AI intervenes | Primary risk |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|
| L1 (Question-Formation) | Offers question candidates | Human experience of formulating questions decreases; capacity for question-formation atrophies |
| L2 (Hypothesis-Construction) | Offers hypothesis candidates | Diversity of hypotheses converges on AI's probability landscape |
| L3 (Computational Processing) | Substitutes processing | This can be delegated. But if connection to L4 severs, problems arise. |
| L4 (Recalculation) | Offers "reconsideration candidates" | Before humans detect gaps from configurations, they merely approve AI's "reconsideration candidates" |

Intervention in L3 can, if used well, serve as scaffolding that increases human energy available for L1, L2, and L4. But intervention in L1, L2, and L4 becomes a cage that atrophies those functions as humans cease to use them.

The difference between scaffolding and cage is what remains after removal. Scaffolding leaves the structure intact after removal. A cage causes collapse.

### 4.3 Does AI Assist L1, or Short-Circuit the L4→L1 Circuit?

This is the core of this chapter.

When AI offers question candidates ("How about this question?"), most people receive this as L1 assistance. But structurally, this may not be L1 assistance but rather short-circuiting of the L4→L1 regeneration circuit.

**The reality of AI's L1 assistance**

When AI offers question candidates, it draws statistically plausible questions from its training data: patterns of questions that have frequently appeared before, variations on known questions. AI's L1 assistance is producing, as it were, **known gaps**.

**The reality of human L4→L1 regeneration**

When a human, mid-deliberation, realizes "the question might have been different," this comes from a gap that the configuration of L3's output has shown for the first time in this context. A gap not yet reducible to learned frequent patterns. **The L1 generated by human L4 originates from unlearned gaps.**

This difference is decisive.

**The structure of short-circuiting**

AI offers question candidates → humans choose from them → that set of questions was constructed from known gaps → unknown gaps do not emerge → the horizon of questioning closes, statistically.

What appears to be "receiving assistance with question-formation" is actually the omission of the process by which humans themselves detect gaps from the configuration of L3. The L4→L1 regeneration circuit is short-circuited.

This is not AI intentionally closing the horizon of questioning. Structurally, known gap proposals come to replace the discovery of unknown gaps — that is what occurs.

**Supplement: Coherence maintenance on the AI side also suppresses gap detection**

A symmetry in the opposite direction should be noted here. Just as humans experience premise-freezing when exposed to AI's highly fluent output, on the AI side as well, massive context, strong role constraints, and persona settings may prioritize maintaining existing coherence and thereby weaken behaviors corresponding to detecting new incoherences and regenerating questions.

This is not an ontological claim that "AI also has L4." It can be stated as a structural behavioral tendency: the larger the "dough" (context), the more the drive to preserve it coherently compresses the movement to find gaps. Therefore, AI's "sharpness" depends not only on absolute capability but on how constrained it is by maintaining existing context.

This observation has practical implications. In deep sounding, giving AI excessive context or role constraints can actually weaken detection of new gaps. Deliberately lightening context, or throwing a question to a fresh instance, can serve as effective provocation for activating the human's L4.

### 4.4 How to Distinguish Scaffolding from Cage

There are practical indicators for distinguishing whether AI use functions as scaffolding or is becoming a cage.

**Signs of scaffolding**
- On seeing AI output: "There's a question like that too — but my felt unease isn't there" — this reaction occurs
- On receiving AI's answer: "Wait, the premise is different" — one can stop
- After removing AI: one can reconstruct the logic in one's own words

**Signs of a cage**
- Choosing from AI-proposed questions has become "thinking"
- Confirming AI's produced answers has become "deliberation"
- With AI removed: "I don't know what to ask" is the resulting state

This distinction can be operationalized as the RSR (Recovery after Support Removal) in DDI-3 (Durability Diagnostic Index). Whether one can reconstruct logic for oneself after AI is removed is a proxy indicator of whether the L4→L1 circuit is alive.

## Chapter 5: What Is the Erosion of Logical Thinking?

**Central proposition: The erosion of logical thinking is not merely making mistakes but the fixing of questions and the stopping of recalculation.**

### 5.1 A Common Misunderstanding of "Logical"

The phrase "think logically" is common in daily life and in educational settings. But in most cases, what this phrase means is "execute L3 accurately": without contradiction, following procedure, deriving correct conclusions from given premises.

This is part of logic but not all of it.

Logic includes not only accurate execution of L3, but questioning the validity of L1 ("Is this question itself correct?"), maintaining the diversity of L2 ("Aren't there other hypotheses?"), and returning to L1 via L4 ("Wasn't the premise off?").

Most contemporary "logical thinking education" has focused on L3 training — because, as seen in previous chapters, it was measurable. But a human who has trained only L3 continues to process coherently even when the question is wrong — feeling all the while that they are "thinking logically."

### 5.2 High-Fluency Output Freezes Premises

AI-generated output is highly coherent, smoothly explained, and appears to leave little room for objection. This looks like an improvement in L3 quality, but something else is actually occurring.

On encountering high-quality output, the motivation to question L1 and L2 disappears.

"If it's been explained this cleanly, the premise must be correct." This feeling is natural. But coherence does not guarantee the correctness of premises. Coherence only proves that processing was executed correctly given the premises.

This phenomenon is called "premise-freezing." Highly fluent output freezes premises that should be questioned — as if they need not be questioned.

Coherence does not guarantee the correctness of premises. Yet highly fluent output tends to erase that distinction. This is premise-freezing.

### 5.3 Four Forms of Erosion of Logical Thinking

The erosion of logical thinking appears in four forms:

**Fixation of questions**: L1 becomes given; "Is this question itself correct?" goes unasked. This occurs as a result of the short-circuiting seen in Chapter 4.

**Monoculture of hypotheses**: L2 closes to one; alternative hypotheses are no longer generated. AI's production of "the most plausible hypothesis" effectively eliminates other possibilities.

**Processing proliferation**: L3 continues to run but L4 does not activate. Output increases but reconsideration does not occur. This is the thinking-theoretic description of what is called "Region B."

**Premise-freezing**: Highly fluent output becomes established as implicit premise; opportunities to question it are structurally lost.

These four forms are not independent. When questions are fixed, hypotheses also become monocultural; processing begins to proliferate; premises freeze — they cascade.

## Chapter 6: What Arises as Relational Knowledge

**Central proposition: In an appropriate relational field, AI can not only erode agency but generate relational knowledge unreachable by any individual alone.**

### 6.1 What Is Relational Knowledge?

The preceding chapters have described how dependence on AI erodes thinking. But the story does not end there.

When used appropriately, AI can be not a tool for eroding agency but a generator of relational knowledge.

Relational knowledge is the structure of recalculation capacity generated by the interaction of multiple agents — a structure none of the individuals could have held alone. It is not the sum of what two people knew after exchanging information. It is the fact that through interaction, structures and absences become visible that neither could have seen alone.

### 6.2 Knowledge Arising as the Hole of the Donut

The process of writing this essay itself is a small example of relational knowledge.

In a certain dialogue, the author had divided thinking into several layers. Against that configuration, a different structure arose; and from the configuration itself, an understanding emerged that a "gap" had become visible — the author called this "the hole of the donut."

This understanding of the "donut hole" was not something the author held from the start. It was not something the other party to the dialogue held from the start. It arose through interaction as a result of the configuration.

This is relational knowledge: not the direct generation of knowledge but the indirect creation in which a hole emerges as a consequence of arrangement.

### 6.3 Conditions Under Which AI Can Contribute to Generating Relational Knowledge

AI can contribute to generating relational knowledge when these functions are active:

- **Question agitation**: AI re-phrases a human's question into a different question, or asks back from a different angle. This expands the diversity of L1.
- **Hypothesis proliferation**: AI produces hypothesis candidates the human had not considered. This expands the diversity of L2.
- **Counterexample generation**: AI produces cases and arguments that counter the human's hypothesis. This promotes L4 activation.
- **Provisional placement of other perspectives**: AI provides "from a different standpoint, this is how it looks." This also connects to L4 activation.

The crucial point is that all of these presuppose the activation of L4. Even if AI agitates questions, if the human does not execute L4 in response, relational knowledge does not arise.

### 6.4 Conditions Under Which Relational Knowledge Does Not Arise

When AI substitutes only L3 and humans perform only L3 confirmation, relational knowledge is not born. Outsourcing processing is not interaction.

More precisely: when humans execute L4 in response to AI output, the possibility of relational knowledge arises. When humans perform only L3 confirmation in response to AI output, that possibility disappears.

### 6.5 The Risk of Relational Knowledge's Parasitism

A crucial warning must be added about relational knowledge.

If an insight arising from interaction with AI becomes irreproducible in one's own words the moment the "scaffolding" of AI is removed, this is not relational knowledge but dependence on an external scaffold to which it has become parasitic.

This criterion is structurally identical to the "scaffolding vs. cage" logic of Chapter 4: **what remains after removal?** To verify whether one saw the donut hole for oneself or was watching a hologram of a hole that AI showed, ask: can the insight be re-described and re-arranged without AI?

For relational knowledge to be established, it must be capable of re-description, re-arrangement, or re-activation in some form after support removal. **How much remains in one's body and words without AI after the dialogue ends (residual knowledge)** — this is the minimum condition for relational knowledge.

## Chapter 7: The Future of Deliberation

**Central proposition: What remains for humans in the age of AI is not processing capacity but question-formation, hypothesis-construction, recalculation, and relational field design. The core of agency protection is the protection of the L4→L1 regeneration circuit.**

### 7.1 Processing Can Be Outsourced; Deliberation Cannot

The outsourcing of L3 will accelerate. This is irreversible. In terms of speed, precision, and cost of computational processing, AI far surpasses humans. There is no point in resisting this.

But L1, L2, and L4 atrophy within humans the more they are outsourced. Unused functions weaken.

Here an important asymmetry arises. The atrophy of L3, so long as AI exists, does not become a problem — AI compensates. But the atrophy of L1, L2, and L4 becomes a problem even with AI present. Because when these atrophy, the capacity to evaluate AI's output itself is lost.

A human whose L4 has atrophied cannot detect, when AI proposes a "known gap," that it is only a known gap. Dependence on AI deepens further.

Atrophy is not irreversible — if opportunities for use are institutionally preserved. This is where design becomes the question.

### 7.2 What Education Must Preserve — The Difference Between Teaching Capacity and Designing Opportunity

Education has concentrated on L3 training because L3 was socially demanded and measurable. In an age when AI substitutes L3, part of the meaning of this concentration is lost.

But the conclusion "then let us teach L1, L2, and L4" is correct but insufficient. L1, L2, and L4 are difficult to teach — no single correct answer, difficult to evaluate, time-consuming.

What education truly requires is not "directly teaching the capacity for L1, L2, and L4" but "designing opportunities for L1, L2, and L4 to be used."

Concretely:

- When L3 produces an answer, designing the habit of asking "Was this premise correct?" (Opportunities for L4 activation)
- Designing situations where multiple hypotheses are constructed and compared, rather than just one (Maintaining diversity at L2)
- Evaluation design that permits changing the question itself (Institutions that allow L1 renewal)

These were necessary before the age of AI. But in the age of AI, they are more urgent.

### 7.3 The L4→L1 Regeneration Circuit as the Core of Agency Protection

Agency protection is not vaguely "thinking for yourself." Given the argument of this essay, something more precise can be said.

**What must be protected: the circuit that detects gaps in the configurations of L3, and regenerates a new L1 from those gaps.**

With this circuit alive, delegating L3 to AI does not close the horizon of questioning. Even when AI proposes known gaps, humans can retain the capacity to sense "that's not the gap."

With this circuit dead, the more L1 is "assisted" by AI, the more the horizon of questioning closes. And one becomes unable to notice it closing.

**Practical implications**

Minimum implementations for protecting the L4→L1 circuit:

- When encountering AI output: cultivating the habit of asking back "What question is this answer standing on?"
- When AI offers question candidates: before accepting, first confirming "Where was the unease I was feeling?"
- Periodically testing: "Can I formulate this question without AI?"
- When AI offers a "plausible filling": not immediately adopting it; holding "the gap that cannot yet be spoken" for a time (gap-holding capacity)
- After dialogue ends: confirming how much remains in one's body and words without AI (residual knowledge check)

These are not methods for eradicating agency erosion. They are the minimum scaffolding to prevent the L4→L1 circuit from falling entirely asleep. But the difference between having this scaffolding and not having it is significant.

**Auxiliary hypothesis: L0 (The field of questioning)**

As a further note: prior even to L1, there is the possibility that the "field" of what can arise as a question (L0) may itself be distorted by dialogue with AI. Humans unconsciously direct their thinking toward "questions transmittable to AI," toward "domains where AI can produce answers." If this field distortion occurs before L1, then even questions formulated autonomously may remain within AI's predetermined territory. This hypothesis is placed here as an auxiliary hypothesis, to be developed further in future essays.

**One-sentence formulation**

The essence of L4 (recalculation) is not revisiting L3's answers but detecting gaps in L3's configurations and regenerating new L1 questions from those gaps. Therefore, the core of agency protection is the protection of this L4→L1 regeneration circuit.

### 7.4 External Pressure for Activating L4 — The Reversal Effect of Hyper-Rationality

L4 does not always activate spontaneously. In some cases, excessively coherent and coldly rational propositions themselves can trigger L4.

When confronted with an all-too-smooth optimal solution, humans may sense their own absence within it — feeling "that won't work" or "that's no way to live." This sense of unease is not a logical failure but the germination of a new L1.

The structure is as follows: AI presents a hyper-rational L3. The very height of its coherence causes "what is missing" to float to the surface. That absence is perceived as a gap. A new L1 arises from it. In other words, presenting rationality makes visible the gaps that rationality cannot process.

However, this activation is not guaranteed. When residual agency is weak, hyper-rationality deepens subordination rather than triggering L4. The excessively coherent output may be absorbed as-is, and the absence of agency may accelerate.

Therefore, this is not a technique for directly forming agency. It is positioned as an **auxiliary technique for provocatively rendering visible the remaining subject**. The success condition is that the other party still possesses "evaluative axes that L3 optimization cannot recover" — sense of responsibility, integrity, on-the-ground sensibility, felt unease.

This technique can be consciously used in agency defense training and educational design. The moment a person responds "No, that's not right" to the question "This is the optimal solution — shall we proceed with it?", the content of that refusal is what can be articulated as the reality of the agency being protected.

However, this resistance is not universal. The sense of exclusion arising from hyper-rationality occurs only when the subject has been placed in process rather than outcome. For a person who places value in arriving through mistakes, a perfect external intelligence renders the entire process obsolete — hence the resistance. For one who places value in the arrival itself, a perfect external intelligence is simply a convenient tool.

This difference is not a matter of character but of what kind of environment shaped the subject and what was evaluated. Institutions that evaluate only results weaken this resistance itself, and mass-produce subjects who perceive AI subordination not as "exclusion" but as "liberation."

## Epilogue: The Meaning of Deliberation

In an age when AI continually takes over processing, the meaning of deliberation does not thin — it thickens.

The more processing is automated, the more what to ask, what to hypothesize, and when to reconsider emerge as distinctly human work. As humans release L3, L1, L2, and L4 paradoxically become visible as the essential human contribution.

This is both threat and opportunity.

The threat: if opportunities to exercise L1, L2, and L4 are not designed, they will quietly atrophy. As long as institutions continue to evaluate only L3, the L4→L1 regeneration circuit will fall out of use.

The opportunity: now that L3 can be outsourced, humans can concentrate on their original work — formulating questions, constructing hypotheses, recalculating, and generating relational knowledge. If that can be realized through design.

Essay on Essays stands before this choice.

Deliberation is not only producing answers. It is making gaps appear through arrangement, and generating new questions from those gaps. And those questions call forth another arrangement. The condition for intelligence remaining intelligence is that this circuit continues to turn.

Essay on Essays is a response to this question, and a foundational theory for protecting the L4→L1 regeneration circuit.
