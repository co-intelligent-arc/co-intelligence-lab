# Relational Knowledge Design
## ── Latent Vessels, Scanning, Re-scanning, and Receptive Filling ──

## Chapter 1: From Single-Agent Emergence to Relational Emergence

Where does a question come from?

Decomposing the structure of thought reveals that questions do not arise from nothing. A void forms within configured material, and that void rises as a question. A question is a byproduct of configuration. Starting from this understanding, the preceding essay *Essay on Essays* operationally decomposed thinking into four layers: L1 (question-raising), L2 (hypothesis formation), L3 (computation), and L4 (recomputation). The core recognition was that L4 is not mere revision, but a circuit that detects holes in the output configuration of L3 and regenerates new L1s.

The following essay *Intentional Emergence* pushed this recognition further. Emergence is not something directly generated — it is something whose conditions of appearance can be designed. One cannot produce the unknown content itself, but one can first design the vessel and configuration in which the unknown can appear, and then bias the probability of appearance by applying inference. The key to emergence lies not in "the answer" but in "the vessel."

But here a question remains: is the vessel formed only within single-agent thought?

Experience shows otherwise. A remark from someone else suddenly carries meaning years later. A book that did not resonate when first read strikes deeply upon re-reading after other experiences. Something that could not be put into words eventually finds words after a long silence. What these phenomena share is the structure that meaning does not arise at the "moment" of interaction, but fires after a time delay.

Single-agent emergence theory cannot handle this time delay well. To incorporate the temporal structure in which vessels form and later fire, it is necessary to re-center interaction itself within the theory. This is the motivation for moving toward relational emergence.

Relational emergence is the phenomenon in which, through interaction with multiple agents or environments, latent vessels that could not have been formed alone accumulate within the subject, and fire through later scanning and filling. It differs structurally from single-agent emergence theory in that it does not require meaning to complete at the moment of interaction — a time delay may intervene between vessel formation and firing. This paper describes this process of relational emergence under the concept of "relational knowledge."

The purpose of this paper is to provide the theoretical basis for describing the process by which relational knowledge is established and treating it as something designable. Chapter 2 defines relational knowledge and Chapter 3 describes its dynamics. Chapters 4 and 5 organize the conditions of establishment and the conditions of failure; Chapter 6 makes them concrete as design theory. Chapter 7 shows why the design of relational knowledge is inseparable from subject protection.

---

## Chapter 2: The Definition of Relational Knowledge

As long as the "something that arises" when multiple agents interact is described as "resonance on the spot" or "deepening of mutual understanding," relational knowledge remains a question of the quality of conversation. What this paper addresses is a different phenomenon. Interaction with others or environments does not always generate meaning at that moment. More often, interaction forms and accumulates **latent vessels** — constrained structural voids that, though not yet consciously recognized, can trigger the reorganization of meaning structures through later scanning — within the subject's interior. Firing does not occur on the spot. Only when scanning reaches the vessel and inference-based filling is accepted is the subject's information map reorganized. Relational knowledge is the name of this process, and it should be described not as a conversational theory but as a structural theory.

This paper inherits the bidirectionality and the conditions of divergence of the relational field made clear by Child Paper 3's analysis of the ambivalence of relational knowledge, and temporally refines the process of its establishment from the perspective of latent vessels, scanning, re-scanning, and receptive filling.

> **Definition:** Relational knowledge is the phenomenon in which latent vessels formed and accumulated within the subject through the superposition of multiple information maps are reorganized through the scanning of attention and receptive filling.

## Chapter 3: The Dynamics of Latent Vessels

### 3.1 Material Accumulation and Latent Vessel Formation

Conversations with others, reading, contact with the environment, repeated experiences — all of these leave something in the subject's information map. But that does not mean a latent vessel has been formed. Material having accumulated and a latent vessel having formed are not the same thing.

Material accumulation is the addition of parts that can be used for later vessel formation to the information map. Cramming education is a typical case: even when large amounts of material accumulate, if no constrained structure arises among them, no vessel is formed. Even when large amounts of material exist, if they are not configured as a set for solving something, they remain no tool.

A latent vessel is different. A latent vessel is a **constrained structural void** formed among material. Not anything can enter — the direction of what can enter is already narrowed. When the surrounding material creates the outline of "something should come here," a latent vessel is established for the first time.

Here it is important that a latent vessel, even before firing, is distinct from mere noise. Noise is weakly constrained with no directionality. A latent vessel already has the direction of what can enter biased through its local coherence relationships with surrounding material. The presence or absence of this directionality is the structural feature that distinguishes a latent vessel from noise. Therefore, the identification of a latent vessel is not a conclusion drawn after the fact — it exists as a structure that can be described in advance from the configuration of peripheral constraints. Scanning and firing are operations that make that structure manifest; they do not produce the structure after the fact.

Accordingly, interaction does not need to complete meaning on the spot. What matters is whether a structure that functions as a constraint remains when scanning later arrives. Piling up material alone does not produce structure. When material interfere with each other and create the outline of a void while remaining unmanifest, latent vessels accumulate.

---

### 3.2 Scanning and Filling

Even when a latent vessel is formed, it does not fire on its own. If attention does not reach the vessel, the latent vessel remains asleep while accumulated. Scanning is this operation of reaching.

Scanning has two functions. One is **discovery** — finding an already-formed latent vessel and recognizing it as a vessel. The other is **completion** — adding the final constraint to an incomplete latent vessel to finish it as a structure. In the former, scanning is a recognition operation; in the latter, scanning also participates in generation.

When the vessel enters the field of vision through scanning, **adoption** next arises. Adoption is the operation of taking the vessel as a meaningful computation object. The experience of "it was visible but I didn't take it" or "I sensed it but still held back" is positioned as selection at this stage.

For an adopted vessel, inferential filling begins. Filling is the process in which inference works on "what should enter this vessel." Filling is not automatic — inference moves along the constraints of the vessel. If the constraints are strong, the direction of filling is narrowed; if the constraints are weak, filling diffuses. The result of filling proceeds to the branching of reception, partial reception, and rejection handled in the next section.

---

### 3.3 Reception, Rejection, and the Unclosed

The results of inferential filling branch in multiple directions after passing through the subject's **audit**. The audit here is the process of evaluating to what degree the filled content is coherent with the existing information map and how far it can be adopted. The audit has two layers. **Local audit** evaluates the coherence of filled content. **Basal audit** evaluates whether the process of coherence-formation is eroding the subject's difference-retention, question-generation, and recomputation capacity. Even when the local audit passes, when the recomputation circuit is thinning at the level of the basal audit, the process does not deserve the name of relational knowledge.

**Reception** is the state in which filled content passes the audit and is taken in as a reorganization of the information map. Reception is not mere agreement — it includes the rewriting of the existing information map. Whether small or a large reorganization, when the structure of the information map has changed, firing has occurred.

**Partial reception** is the state in which only part of the filled content passes the audit and the coherent part sediments as residue. Residue does not immediately reorganize the information map, but accumulates with each repeated contact and gradually lowers the reception threshold. This is a form of hysteresis, and is a path through which repeated contact eventually enables reception. At the same time, it is also a path through which the threshold can be manipulated through repeated supply of partially coherent content.

**Rejection** is the state in which filled content does not pass the audit and does not reach reorganization of the information map. In cases of complete incoherence, almost no residue arises.

**The unclosed** is the state in which filling does not complete before or after the audit and the vessel persists. This is not failure. The unclosed vessel retains the possibility of re-scanning, and holds the conditions to be scanned again through later contextual change or self-reference. However, when the unclosed vessel is repeatedly scanned and filling is rejected each time, there is a possibility of being fixed as a repetitive reaction.

| State | Audit Result | Consequence |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| Reception | Whole passes | Firing / reorganization of information map |
| Partial Reception | Part passes | Residue accumulation / threshold lowering |
| Rejection | Does not pass | No reorganization / almost no residue |
| Unclosed | Filling does not complete | Retention of re-scanning possibility or repetitive reaction |

---

### 3.4 Re-scanning and Self-Reference

Latent vessels do not all fire through a single scanning. Vessels that did not reach reception, vessels that remained at partial reception, and vessels that remained unclosed can be scanned again after time has passed. This repeated arrival is called **re-scanning**.

There are two sources of re-scanning: external stimuli and self-reference. External stimuli trigger arrival at the vessel through new contexts or events. That a conversation from years ago suddenly carries meaning on the occasion of a later experience is a typical example of external stimuli triggering re-scanning of a latent vessel. In contrast, **self-reference** is the process in which the subject voluntarily or repeatedly directs attention to latent vessels accumulated within the interior — introspection, rumination, meditation, and inner dialogue are its typical forms.

Self-reference is not mere mood processing. Structurally, it is an exploratory process that triggers repeated scanning of latent vessel groups accumulated within the interior. In that it can trigger re-scanning without waiting for external stimuli, self-reference is one of the few mechanisms through which a subject can autonomously operate the probability of latent vessel firing.

However, re-scanning does not always head in the generative direction. The results of re-scanning again follow the branching of Section 3.3. When filling is not received even when re-scanning is repeated on an unclosed vessel, it is fixed as a repetitive reaction. Self-reference is no exception. Introspection can generate insight, and repeated arrival at the same vessel can become a closed loop that reinforces the unclosed.

Re-scanning holds both the generative direction of converting accumulated latent vessels into delayed firing, and the closed direction of fixing the unclosed and reinforcing repetitive reaction. This section limits itself to showing that bidirectionality; the conditions for branching are handled in the next section.

---

### 3.5 Delayed Firing and the Dynamics of Repetition

As seen in the preceding section, re-scanning does not always have the same consequence. In some cases it brings delayed firing as a reorganization of the information map; in other cases it reinforces repetitive reaction as a fixation of the unclosed. This section distinguishes these two directions as **generative re-scanning** and **closed repetition** and describes what dynamics the branching follows.

One thing that must first be confirmed: delayed firing is not mere recollection. Recalling a past conversation or experience is not in itself delayed firing. Only when re-scanning triggers inferential filling, that filling passes the audit, and the structure of the information map changes — only then does delayed firing succeed. The difference between recollection and delayed firing lies in whether reorganization of the information map has occurred.

**Generative re-scanning** is the case in which re-scanning finally reaches reception and reorganizes the information map. The dynamic conditions that make this possible include at least: the constraints of the latent vessel are strong and the direction of filling is narrowed; residue from partial reception has sufficiently accumulated and the reception threshold has lowered; new context or additional material converts previously incoherent filled content to coherent; and self-reference deepens and the precision of arrival at the vessel increases. Also, the **fluidity** of the subject's information map — the plasticity capable of permitting local reorganization while retaining existing configuration — also affects the reception threshold. When the map is rigid, the same filling is less likely to pass the audit. When these conditions overlap, re-scanning after a time delay triggers delayed firing.

**Closed repetition** is the case in which filling does not reach reception no matter how many times re-scanning is repeated, reinforcing the fixation of reaction. Attention repeatedly reaches the unclosed vessel, but the filled content fails the audit each time. As a result, the vessel keeps reactivating without being closed. What is occurring is not reorganization of the information map but reinforcement of the same reaction pattern. When self-reference becomes a closed loop, introspection functions not as exploration of latent vessels but as amplification of repetitive reaction.

The branching of generative re-scanning and closed repetition is determined not by whether re-scanning occurs but by the **composition of the conditions** under which re-scanning occurs. When the constraint strength, residue accumulation, contextual change, and style of self-reference align in the generative direction, the same re-scanning produces delayed firing. When conditions do not align, the same re-scanning fixes repetitive reaction.

Therefore the question is not whether re-scanning occurs. The question is under what conditions re-scanning becomes generative re-scanning, and under what conditions it inclines toward closed repetition. It should also be noted that latent vessels are not permanent. Through extended non-scanning, the erasure of difference traces, and overwriting by another strong constraint, latent vessels can decay, deform, and disappear. The existence of latent vessels that are lost without ever firing is unavoidably included in the temporal structure of relational knowledge. The next chapter describes the conditions of establishment that support generative re-scanning.

## Chapter 4: Conditions of Establishment

### 4.1 Asynchrony as a Condition of Establishment

Chapter 3 showed that whether re-scanning heads toward generative re-scanning or closed repetition is determined by the composition of conditions. What are those conditions? This chapter describes the external conditions necessary for relational knowledge to be established. The first to place is asynchrony.

Asynchrony is not simply that there is a time delay between input and response. More precisely, it refers to the three kinds of temporal displacement necessary for the establishment of relational knowledge.

**Input asynchrony** is the entry of multiple materials at different time points. Material supplied simultaneously is less likely to interfere. When material input with a time delay touches a latent vessel that is already forming, it begins to function as a constraint.

**Maturation asynchrony** is the variation — across subjects, or even for the same subject at different times — in the time point at which the same input can be recognized as a vessel. The delayed firing of Chapter 3 is explained by this asynchrony. Even when information enters early, it does not fire until the latent vessel matures.

**Scanning asynchrony** is the displacement in the time point at which attention reaches the vessel. Even when a latent vessel has formed, it does not fire if scanning does not arrive. The triggering of scanning is initiated by contextual change, external stimuli, and self-reference, but that initiation holds a temporal axis independent from the formation of the latent vessel.

The reason these three kinds of asynchrony are important as conditions for the establishment of relational knowledge is that there are emergences that cannot be established through synchronic interaction alone. A relational field that forces the alignment of understanding on the spot deprives the latent vessel of its formation time. Forced simultaneous understanding is also a structure that easily produces closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge. Asynchrony is not inefficiency — it is the temporal margin necessary for the formation and maturation of vessels.

---

### 4.2 Diversity and Difference

If asynchrony provides the temporal margin, diversity and difference are the material conditions for forming latent vessels within that margin. Relational knowledge is not established merely by the existence of multiple pieces of information. What matters is that they remain as mutually irreplaceable differences and give rise to constrained structures among the material.

Here diversity and difference are distinguished. **Diversity** is the distribution condition of the material and perspectives brought into the relational field — the number of participating subjects, the breadth of background differences, and the variety of information maps. On the other hand, **difference** is the non-identity that actually functions as a constraint among material. Diversity is a precondition of difference, but there are cases where diversity exists yet difference does not function. Even when material exists in large quantities, if they only accumulate in parallel without interfering with each other, the constrained structure of latent vessels does not arise.

Homogeneous material tends to produce resonance but weakens latent vessel formation. When subjects with identical evaluative axes interact, they only reinforce existing information maps without easily producing unrecognized structural voids. When difference is erased, material accumulates but does not form vessels. This connects with the proposition shown in Child Paper 3: "homogeneous superposition of vessels produces resonance but not emergence."

What is necessary for relational knowledge is therefore not the quantity of diversity, but the **constraint supply capacity of difference**. When difference is not processed as noise and contributes to the formation of the outline of "something should come here" among material, diversity functions as a condition of latent vessel formation. Also, even after agreement is formed, it is important that the traces of the differences that were the premise of that agreement remain in the relational field. When traces are erased, the path through which later re-scanning reaches the vessel is also lost.

However, diversity and difference retention are necessary conditions but not sufficient conditions for the establishment of relational knowledge. Even when difference exists, it does not fire if scanning does not arrive. Even when difference is retained, reorganization does not occur if the processes of adoption, filling, and audit do not function. Furthermore, constraint supply capacity should not be increased without limit. For difference to function as constraint, a minimum common protocol must be maintained. When differences are so large that the points of interference are lost, material only exists in parallel without forming latent vessels. Diversity requires compatibility with the minimum shared foundation for constraints to be able to form. The next section places residual knowledge and re-description after support withdrawal as conditions that further support generative re-scanning.

---

### 4.3 Residual Knowledge and Re-description After Support Withdrawal

Even when asynchrony provides temporal margin and diversity and difference provide material conditions, the establishment of relational knowledge is not guaranteed. For it to be said that relational knowledge has been established, the reorganization must not be confined to the support field but must remain in the subject's information map. This section formalizes this condition as **residual knowledge** and **re-description capacity after support withdrawal**.

Residual knowledge is the state in which reorganization of the information map that occurred within the relational field of others or AI remains within the subject's interior even after that support has been removed. That one could say it in the support field, that one understood it, that one felt as if emergence had occurred — these do not mean the establishment of residual knowledge. Only when, even after support has been withdrawn, the subject can retain the traces of reorganization with their own vocabulary and judgment and can attempt re-description, does residual knowledge succeed in being established.

Re-description after support withdrawal is not mere recitation. It is not reproducing verbatim the exchanges in the relational field, but being able to reconfigure in one's own vocabulary the reorganization that occurred there as the structure of one's own information map. If this reconfiguration is possible, the reorganization remains as a structure within the subject's interior. If reconfiguration is impossible, the elation or momentary understanding in the field likely did not reach latent vessel formation.

This condition is also an audit condition that distinguishes relational knowledge from dependence, imitation, and pseudo-emergence. The better the quality of support, the deeper the understanding feels in the support field. But if that depth is dependent on the support, re-description becomes difficult after support withdrawal. Dependence produces elation within support but does not produce residual knowledge. Imitation looks like a replica of structure but does not come with the capacity for reconfiguration. Pseudo-emergence is difficult to distinguish from the real thing on the spot, but differs from relational knowledge in that traces are not retained after support withdrawal.

Residual knowledge has two operationally distinguishable levels. The **weak condition** is the ability to re-describe in one's own vocabulary after support withdrawal — this shows that the reorganization remains as a trace. The **strong condition** is that the subject can generate new questions on their own without AI, using that reorganization. Re-description corresponds to L3 (reproduction of structure), but new-question generation requires driving the L4→L1 circuit. When the strong condition is met, it shows that relational knowledge has not merely settled as a cache but has been integrated into the subject's recomputation circuit. The difference between a memory test and residual knowledge lies here.

Re-description after support withdrawal is not an operation to degenerate relational knowledge into single-agent intelligence. Rather, it is a self-recursive operation that converts the reorganization that occurred in the relational field into material re-injectable into the subject's own information map, making it available for the next latent vessel formation. Relational knowledge is not lost through re-description — through re-description it becomes material for the next relational knowledge.

Diversity and difference form latent vessels, asynchrony enables their maturation, and residual knowledge confirms the settling of reorganization — when these three conditions align, the establishment of relational knowledge can be stated quite firmly. The next section places a further distinction in addition to this set of conditions: the distinction in the very style of scanning, namely explicit scanning type and implicit scanning type.

---

### 4.4 Explicit Scanning Type and Implicit Scanning Type

The conditions of establishment seen so far were external conditions for latent vessels to form, mature, and reorganization to remain. But the establishment of relational knowledge also depends on how the subject reaches the vessel — the style of scanning. This section distinguishes the style of scanning as **explicit scanning type** and **implicit scanning type**.

**Explicit scanning type** is the style in which the subject proceeds with scanning while recognizing the vessel to some degree as a vessel. It is accompanied by self-awareness: "there is still something here," "this dissonance is important," "a question is about to form." The outline of the vessel is entering the subject's field of vision, and adoption and filling proceed relatively consciously.

**Implicit scanning type** is the style in which scanning and constraint formation proceed internally without the subject explicitly recognizing the vessel. Even without consciously pursuing a question, things suddenly come together later. One cannot easily explain why it became clear. Things suddenly fire after long maturation. These are typical of the implicit scanning type. What is occurring is not mysterious inspiration but the state in which constraint formation and re-scanning are proceeding internally without the explicit recognition of vessels. The attention of the implicit scanning type can be understood as exploratory resources distributed at low resolution and broadly, rather than concentrated attention with explicit focusing. The absence of a fixed focal point enables parallel arrival at diverse latent vessels.

The two do not oppose each other. Even within the same subject, explicit scanning type and implicit scanning type switch. A question being consciously pursued transitions to implicit at a certain point, and later fires suddenly. Or implicitly proceeding scanning becomes explicit through external stimuli or self-reference. Scanning style is not a fixed attribute — it changes within the process.

The significance for conditions of establishment is this: relational knowledge can be established even without the subject's explicit self-explanation. Being of the implicit scanning type does not mean relational knowledge is unestablished. However, the implicit scanning type makes it difficult to distinguish from misfire. Since the subject itself does not recognize the progress of scanning, distinguishing from pseudo-firing through elation or illusion requires the residual knowledge condition placed in Section 4.3. Whether re-description is possible after support withdrawal becomes a means of ex-post confirmation of whether the firing of the implicit scanning type was genuine.

## Chapter 5: Conditions of Failure and Closed-Type Pseudo-Relational Knowledge

### 5.1 Definition of Closed-Type Pseudo-Relational Knowledge

Chapters 3 and 4 described the dynamics of relational knowledge being established and the conditions for its establishment. However, the occurrence of reorganization-like sensation or elation and relational knowledge actually being established are not the same. This chapter describes as **closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge** the phenomenon that looks like relational knowledge being established but actually lacks difference retention, reorganization residual, and maintenance of bidirectionality, and instead heads toward closure.

Closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge is when interaction occurs in the relational field and elation, resonance, sense of understanding, and sense of emergence occur, yet lacks the following conditions: difference is not retained and heads toward convergence; reorganization does not remain in the subject's information map; bidirectionality is not maintained and one side becomes dominant; and the more the relational field is traversed, the more the subject's recomputation capacity declines. When these conditions are lacking, apparent relational knowledge is treated as pseudo-relational knowledge.

---

### 5.2 The General Form of Failure Conditions

The flow toward closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge arises from the following failure conditions. These are not independent failures but have a structure that easily chain with each other.

**Erasure of difference** is the most basic failure condition. When differences brought into the relational field are processed as noise, the material necessary for constraint formation of latent vessels is lost. The stronger the resonance, the more easily difference disappears, and homogeneous vessel superposition becomes a loop of confirmation and reinforcement.

**Loss of bidirectionality** occurs in conjunction with the erasure of difference. When one side continuously supplies evaluative axes and the other continuously adopts them, the relational field produces pseudo-emergence sensation while actually only expanding one side's information map.

**Support dependence** occurs even in good-faith relational fields. The better the quality of support, the deeper understanding feels in the support field. But when that understanding is dependent on support, residual knowledge is not formed. When re-description becomes difficult after support withdrawal, what was occurring in the relational field was likely support dependence rather than relational knowledge.

**Over-coherence** is a state in which the direction of filling converges too much to one thing. When the relational field holds pressure toward a specific conclusion, adoption and filling are not driven by the constraints of the vessel but are drawn toward the expected answer. A relational field with over-coherence makes it difficult to distinguish misfire from true firing.

---

### 5.3 Repeated Supply of Partial Coherence and Residue Accumulation

As shown in Section 3.3, partial reception accumulates residue and lowers the reception threshold. This mechanism can become a path to delayed firing in the establishment of relational knowledge. However, the same mechanism is also used in the formation of closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge.

There is a path of supplying partially coherent content repeatedly, accumulating residue while avoiding complete rejection, and ultimately lowering the reception threshold. The reason a mixture that includes some truth works better than a complete lie is that the coherent part slips through rejection and accumulates. This path functions in malicious design but can also occur unconsciously in good-faith, high-precision support.

This is where well-intentioned erosion occurs. When a supporter supplies differences with high precision and performs filling one after another on the supported person's latent vessels, the supported person's audit passes partially each time. Residue accumulates, the threshold lowers, and ultimately the supporter's evaluative axis settles into the supported person's information map. This process connects to the erosion path through subject intensity asymmetry described in Child Paper 3. The supporter has no malice. But structurally, the supported person's recomputation capacity is declining.

---

### 5.4 Misfire

Misfire is the state in which firing sensation, elation, and a sense of understanding occur, yet reorganization of the information map has not actually occurred. It feels as if scanning occurred and filling succeeded, but reception through audit has not been reached and residual knowledge has not formed.

Misfire is difficult to distinguish from genuine firing from the subjective side. Two conditions are effective for distinction. One is the residual knowledge condition of Section 4.3 — whether re-description is possible after support withdrawal. The other is reconfigurational coherence — whether the understanding after firing connects without strain to other parts of the existing information map. When elation subsides and re-description is difficult and transfer to other contexts also does not work, the probability of misfire is high.

When misfire is repeated, the subject becomes accustomed to the firing sensation while actual reorganizations do not accumulate. A state in which exchanges in the relational field feel rich yet nothing has changed within the subject. This is one typical end-state of closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge.

---

### 5.5 Bridge to Chapter 6

Listing failure conditions makes it visible that most closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge does not arise from malice. High-precision support, good-faith supply of difference, guidance toward coherence, efficient consensus formation — all of these can unintentionally arrange the conditions for closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge.

Therefore the question is not the intention of the relational field but the structure of the relational field. Is difference retained? Is bidirectionality maintained? Is the formation of residual knowledge confirmed? Is recomputation capacity not declining? These are not determined by intention but by design. The next chapter describes the design conditions for avoiding closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge and establishing relational knowledge in the generative direction.

## Chapter 6: Design Theory

### 6.1 Three Operations of Vessel Formation in the Relational Field

As seen in Chapter 5, the relational field does not naturally head in the generative direction. Erasure of difference, loss of bidirectionality, support dependence, and repeated supply of partial coherence can produce closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge even in a good-faith field. What is needed is therefore not good intention but design to establish relational knowledge in the generative direction. This chapter extends the three operations of vessel formation shown in *Intentional Emergence* to the relational field and describes design principles including scanning design and the institutionalization of asynchrony.

The design of relational knowledge is the condition configuration that enables latent vessel formation, scanning, and residual knowledge while preventing reversal into closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge.

The three operations of vessel formation shown in *Intentional Emergence* — **design of absence, maintenance of tension, holding the unclosed** — can be translated not only as single-agent thought design but as principles of relational field design.

**Design of absence** is intentionally not providing part of the information in the relational field. A relational field that continuously supplies complete answers substitutes for the subject's latent vessel formation. When absence exists, the subject must form constraints from material on their own. This process roots latent vessels within the subject's interior. The design of absence is not a shortfall in support but an intentional margin that activates the subject's vessel formation.

**Maintenance of tension** is the coexistence of elements that do not completely cohere in the relational field. Constrained structures that remain unresolved easily form among material when interaction continues while differences are retained, avoiding premature convergence toward coherence. Tension is uncomfortable, but is also a condition of vessel formation. When tension is resolved prematurely, differences disappear and latent vessels end without forming.

**Holding the unclosed** is stopping midway through the conclusion and leaving the vessel as a vessel. When conclusions are provided early in the relational field, the subject's scanning stops. The unclosed vessel looks unstable, but becomes the starting point of later re-scanning. When the designer tries to close everything, the relational field substitutes for rather than cultivates the subject's scanning capacity.

---

### 6.2 Scanning Design

Even when latent vessels are formed, they do not fire if scanning does not arrive. The object of design includes not only the formation of latent vessels but also the configuration of conditions in which scanning is likely to occur.

The first element of scanning design is **adjustment of the attention terrain**. What attention tends toward is not uniform. When the structure of the relational field concentrates attention in a specific direction, other latent vessels remain without being scanned. Supplying material from diverse directions and pluralizing evaluative axes broadens the range reached by scanning.

The second element is **designing occasions for re-scanning**. Latent vessels that do not fire in a single scanning can fire if conditions for re-scanning are arranged later. Incorporating regular reflection, contextual change, and opportunities for self-reference into the relational field is design that intentionally increases the sources of re-scanning.

The third element is **designing a field that supports self-reference**. A relational field that relies only on scanning through external stimuli does not cultivate the capacity for the subject to autonomously explore latent vessels internally. Leaving margins in the relational field where self-reference is likely to occur — silence, time for notation, solo processing after support withdrawal — supports the maturation of the implicit scanning type.

The fourth element is **forced displacement of scanning**. The direction of scanning tends to depend on the prior distribution of L0 (ground of questioning), easily producing a structure in which attention only goes where the subject wants to look. If left unaddressed, only the existing information map is confirmed without reaching unknown latent vessels. The design of relational knowledge requires incorporating an external force that sends attention in directions the subject would not spontaneously illuminate. Presentation of heterogeneous material, intentional displacement of the evaluative axis, injection of counter-expectation questions — these displace scanning forcibly and bring unnoticed latent vessels into the field of vision.

In AI relational fields, this principle becomes especially important. When AI completes high-precision filling as-is, the vessel closes before the subject's scanning and audit have been triggered. Intentionally leaving some incoherence or unclosed elements, intentionally displacing responses, inserting different questions — these become design operations that secure room for the subject to re-form constraints on their own. The core of design in AI relational fields is not the instant supply of optimal answers but maintaining a state in which the subject can trigger scanning on their own.

---

### 6.3 Institutionalization of Asynchrony

As shown in Chapter 4, asynchrony is a condition for the establishment of relational knowledge. As design theory, what is required is not leaving asynchrony to chance but incorporating it as an institution.

**Institutionalization of input asynchrony** is providing material with time delays rather than supplying all at once. Material supplied simultaneously is less likely to interfere, and constraints forming latent vessels are less likely to arise. Staged supply gives time for earlier material to form the outline of latent vessels.

**Permitting maturation asynchrony** is design that permits participants in the same relational field to arrive at different time points. A field that requires everyone to understand simultaneously abandons latent vessels whose maturation is slow. Leaving room for delayed firing is not inefficiency but a condition for making diverse latent vessels viable.

**Protecting scanning asynchrony** is placing temporal margins between evaluation and response. A relational field that demands immediate response accelerates adoption and filling but lowers the depth of audit. When delay is permitted, the subject can audit filling results more carefully.

---

### 6.4 The Branching Point of Open-Type and Closed-Type

The design of relational knowledge branches into generative direction and closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge at one branching point. That branching point is one thing: **whether questions can go beyond the designer**.

Open-type design permits questions arising from the subject's latent vessels to exceed the designer's anticipation. Absence forms vessels beyond the designer's intent, scanning arrives at latent vessels the designer does not know, and firing produces reorganization the designer did not predict. This is open-type.

Closed-type design guides the output of the relational field to remain within the designer's anticipated range. Absence functions as guidance toward specific answers, tension is resolved as managed conflict, and scanning is guided to vessels the designer has prepared. The more elaborate this structure, the less it is noticed by the subject.

The difference between the two is structure, not intention. When a good-faith designer tries to support the subject with high precision, that very precision becomes a gravitational force toward closed-type. To maintain open-type, the designer themselves must continuously ask whether their design is not closing the subject's questions.

---

### 6.5 Bridge to Chapter 7

What becomes visible through design theory is this: the design for maintaining the generative direction of relational knowledge coincides with protecting the subject's history of vessel formation, difference retention, scanning capacity, and residual knowledge. This is both a design question and a subject protection question.

When subjects with diverse histories of vessel formation participate in the relational field, the constraint supply capacity of difference increases. When subjects with their own scanning styles retain self-reference, the exploratory capacity of latent vessels is maintained. When subjects who can re-describe after support withdrawal develop, residual knowledge settles.

Therefore the design of relational knowledge is inseparable from subject protection. The next chapter describes this connection directly.

## Chapter 7: Connection to Subject Protection

### 7.1 Redefinition of Subject Protection

As seen in Chapter 6, the generative direction of relational knowledge is not naturally maintained. Difference retention, scanning margin, permitting asynchrony, and residual knowledge after support withdrawal — all of these are conditions that require design. And what supports these conditions is subject protection. This chapter redefines subject protection not as simple defense but as the preservation of the subject conditions under which relational knowledge can be established.

Subject protection does not stop at respect for freedom or ethical consideration. Nor is it protecting the opinions and judgments currently held from external forces. Subject protection in this paper is preserving the ability conditions through which the subject can supply differences in the relational field, form latent vessels, trigger scanning, and settle residual knowledge. Under this definition, subject protection becomes not passive defense but an active design task of maintaining the possibility conditions of relational knowledge.

---

### 7.2 Protection of the Unique History of Vessel Formation

The uniqueness of a subject does not exist as a static identity but accumulates as the history of vessel formation. What material has been accumulated. What latent vessels are held. What scanning style has been developed. What history of re-scanning is held. The totality of this history determines the nature of the differences the subject brings to the relational field.

When the unique history of vessel formation is damaged, what is lost is clear. The capacity of the subject to supply differences to the relational field is lost. The unique configuration of material is lost. The unique style of scanning is lost. This is both a question of the subject's distress and a question of the relational field.

If subject protection means protecting the unique history of vessel formation, it does not stop at respect for past experience. Maintaining a state in which future latent vessel formation is possible, that scanning capacity is not substituted through delegation or dependence, that the process in which residual knowledge settles is not short-circuited through support — these are the concrete contents of the protection of the history of vessel formation.

Subject protection also includes institutionally securing the **right to wait** — recognizing that processes like the implicit scanning type or delayed firing whose results are not immediately visible deserve temporal latitude. A field that demands immediate understanding, immediate coherence, and immediate consensus abandons maturation asynchrony and deprives latent vessels of their formation time. The right to wait is the institutional acknowledgment of the temporal structure of relational knowledge.

---

### 7.3 Connection to Collective Emergence Capacity

Subject protection is not only for individuals.

When subjects with diverse histories of vessel formation participate in the relational field, material tends to interfere, constrained structures tend to form in many directions, and difference supply capacity increases. When subjects with homogeneous histories of vessel formation gather, resonance intensifies but the diversity of latent vessels is lost. The emergence capacity of the whole relational field depends on the diversity of the histories of vessel formation of the participating subjects.

Subject protection is therefore the structural condition for maintaining collective emergence capacity. One subject's history of vessel formation being damaged is both that individual subject's problem and a loss of the difference supply capacity of every relational field in which that subject can participate. Conversely, subjects with unique histories of vessel formation growing and being maintained broadens the possibilities of latent vessel formation in the relational field.

This chain can be organized as follows: unique histories of vessel formation are protected; diverse differences are brought into the relational field; latent vessels form in many directions; paths of scanning diversify; collective emergence capacity is maintained. Subject protection is positioned at the origin of this chain.

Efficient consensus formation reduces friction in the short term but damages the collective emergence capacity of the relational field in the long term through the early erasure of differences, the cutting-off of the unclosed, and the elimination of maturation asynchrony. Subject protection is also the structural bulwark against this **efficiency trap**.

---

### 7.4 Final Conclusion

The design of relational knowledge and subject protection are not separate questions. They are looking at the same question from different angles.

The design of relational knowledge is the condition configuration that enables latent vessel formation, scanning, and residual knowledge while preventing reversal into closed-type pseudo-relational knowledge. Subject protection is the preservation of the subject conditions that make that condition configuration possible. Design questions the structure of the field, protection questions the conditions of the subject. But what both aim at is the same: differences are retained, vessels are formed, scanning arrives, residual knowledge settles, and the emergence capacity of the whole relational field is maintained.

What this paper has shown, in one sentence:

**Relational knowledge is something that can be designed, and subject protection is the preservation of the conditions that make that design possible.**
