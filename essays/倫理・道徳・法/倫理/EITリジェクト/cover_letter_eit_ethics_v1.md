**Cover Letter**

Dear Editors of Ethics and Information Technology,

  


Please consider my manuscript, “Ethics as a Design Principle: Re-Intervention, Circuit Closure, and Human Agency under AI Mediation,” for publication in Ethics and Information Technology.

This paper offers a philosophical reconceptualization of ethics under conditions of AI-mediated decision-making. Rather than treating ethics as a framework for specifying the content of right action, it argues that the prior question under contemporary sociotechnical conditions is what must not be lost if human agency is to remain operative at all. Starting from the minimal condition of re-intervention possibility, the paper derives the following proposition: ethics is a design principle aimed at preventing the closure of the circuit by which a person detects, retains, and transforms the gap between internal ideals and reality.

The manuscript further argues that ethics alone is insufficient for social resilience. A viable social order requires a division of labor among ethics, law and institutions, and morality: ethics protects the circuit of individual re-intervention, law and institutions adjudicate conflicts among circuits, and morality functions as an internal mechanism that reduces divergence prior to coercive adjustment. The paper concludes by connecting this framework to LLM-mediated society, where AI systems can systematically accelerate forms of circuit closure at scale.

I wish to be transparent about the context of this submission. This manuscript is one of three related theoretical papers developed in close succession. A second manuscript, “AI Alignment as the Preservation of the Human Moral Layer: Myopic Error and Recomputation Support,” which extends the present framework to a functional reconceptualization of morality, has been submitted to this journal concurrently. A third manuscript, “Preserving Evaluative Space: The Shepherd Model as a Design Principle for Human Recomputation under AI Mediation,” is currently under editorial assessment at this journal.

These three papers form a connected theoretical series: the ethics paper establishes the minimal design condition for individual agency under AI mediation; the morality paper extends this to the social layer of internal adjustment; and the Shepherd Model paper develops a concrete architectural framework derived from the same theoretical foundations. While each paper is self-contained and independently reviewable, together they constitute a coordinated response to a shared problem: the conditions under which human judgment can remain operative in AI-mediated environments.

I draw explicit attention to the fact that this body of work was developed within a compressed timeframe, with substantial analytical and drafting support from large language models operating under structured research protocols. This is not incidental. The speed and theoretical density with which this series was produced is itself a symptom of the transformation the papers theorize. I believe this context is relevant to how the editorial community may wish to assess and situate work of this kind, and I disclose it in that spirit rather than as a disclaimer.

I believe these manuscripts are well suited to Ethics and Information Technology because they make a philosophical contribution to foundational concepts—ethics, morality, and their relationship to AI design—while remaining grounded in the sociotechnical conditions that make these questions urgent. The journals’ focus on the intersection of ethical theory and information technology aligns precisely with the central concerns of this work.

All three manuscripts are original, have not been published elsewhere, and are not under consideration by any other journal.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
