# EIT_front_matter

**Ethics as a Design Principle:**

**Re-Intervention, Circuit Closure, and Human Agency under AI Mediation**

****  


**Abstract**

This paper reconceptualizes ethics under conditions of AI-mediated decision-making. Existing ethical frameworks, including utilitarian, deontological, and virtue-based approaches, primarily specify the content of right action. Yet under sociotechnical conditions in which large language models increasingly mediate judgment, the prior philosophical question is no longer only what is right, but what must not be lost if human agency is to remain operative at all.

Starting from the minimal condition of re-intervention possibility, the paper derives the following proposition: ethics is a design principle aimed at preventing the closure of the circuit by which a person detects, retains, and transforms the gap between internal ideals and reality. What ethics protects, on this account, is not the substantive content of those ideals, but the continued operability of the circuit through which discrepancy remains active as a condition of human recomputation. Ethics is thus shifted from a theory of correct action to a theory of preserving the functional conditions under which judgment remains possible.

The paper further argues that ethics alone is insufficient. A viable social order requires a division of labor among ethics, law and institutions, and morality: ethics protects the circuit of individual re-intervention, law and institutions adjudicate conflicts among circuits, and morality reduces divergence prior to coercive adjustment. This framework becomes especially urgent in LLM society, where AI-mediated systems can systematically accelerate forms of circuit closure at scale.

**Keywords:** ethics; AI mediation; human agency; re-intervention; recomputation; circuit closure; subject protection
