**Short Title** |  AI Alignment and the Moral Layer  
  
Abstract

This paper redefines the problem of AI alignment not as the moralization of AI outputs but as the preservation of the conditions under which the human moral layer can continue to operate under sustained interaction with AI. Morality has conventionally been treated as a content term associated with goodwill or common sense. In contrast, this paper defines morality functionally as an internal adjustment mechanism that incorporates the sustainability of others, relationships, and the community into one's decision-making, thereby reducing the gap between local and global optima prior to coercive intervention.

The paper further argues that many moral failures arise not primarily from malice but from myopic error, that is, reductions in spatial, temporal, and difficulty range through which interdependence drops out of calculation. AI-mediated systems can either support the recomputation needed to counteract such reductions or accelerate optimization within a narrowed range, thereby externalizing and progressively closing the human recomputation circuit.

The paper repositions morality in the age of AI not as moral content to be preached but as a design principle, and formulates the three-layer structure of ethics, morality, and law as control conditions for optimization schemes in general. This framework inverts the question of AI alignment from the moralization of outputs to the preservation of the human basis for judgment.
